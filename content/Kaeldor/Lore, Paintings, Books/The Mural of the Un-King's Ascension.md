**Appearance:** This is not a carving of stone, but a vast, weeping wound in the ice wall itself. The mural is a fifty-foot expanse of black, corrupted ice, veined with pulsating violet light that shifts and writhes like a living thing. It is not cold to the touch; it radiates a sickening, necrotic warmth, and the air around it smells of ozone and decay. Unlike the patient, deliberate art of the giants, this mural is violent and chaotic, looking less like a creation and more like a psychic scar burned into the heart of their sanctuary. It seems to have appeared recently, a brutal intrusion into the library's sanctity.

**Content:** The scene is a dynamic, horrifying vision of a future apocalypse, its figures subtly moving within the corrupted ice.

At the mural's edges, the giants of [[Old Jotenheim]] are depicted. They are recognizable, but twisted into monstrous parodies of their former glory. Their forms, once like living stone, are cracked and broken, with the same violet energy from the other paintings seeping from the fissures. Their sacred vow of silence is shattered; their mouths are open in eternal, soundless screams of agony. They are not merely fighting; they are being unmade, their individual forms melting away like wax.

As the eye is drawn to the centre, the individual giants dissolve entirely. Their screaming forms flow inward, their substance stone, ice, and soul being drawn into a spiralling vortex. They are not joining an army; they are becoming raw material.

The vortex culminates in a single, triumphant figure: **[[Lich King|Eldhoof]], the [[Lich King]]**. He is no longer the small, shadowy prince. He is a titan of obsidian and malevolence, swollen with the stolen might of the giants. His Armor is not merely worn; it is _made_ of the giants. A pauldron is a giant's screaming face, a greave is a twisted torso, and his flowing cloak is a tapestry of dissolving souls. His crown, once absent, is now a jagged thing of black ice, and from within his helm, two points of violet light burn with annihilating intelligence. He is not just leading an army of the corrupted; he _is_ the corruption, a sentient plague devouring a race to fuel his apotheosis into a new, terrible kind of god.

Behind him, the background shows the ruins of [[Old Jotenheim]] under a black sky, where the "Empty Star" from the astrolabe painting now hangs, no longer a void but a burning, violet eye, gazing down upon its champion's victory.

**Interaction & Mechanics:** This mural is a direct psychic assault, a prophecy that seeks to make itself true by breaking the will of those who view it.

- **The Vision's Pull:** Any character who spends more than a moment looking at the mural must make a **DC 16 Wisdom saving throw**.
- **On a Failure:** The character is stunned for one round as their mind is flooded with a personalized vision. They see not the giants' future, but their own a glimpse of what they would become in service to the [[Lich King]]. [[Blake]] might see himself kneeling, offering a broken Lionheart to his great-uncle. [[Aolis]] could see her faith twisted into a death cult. [[Tyris]] might see his arcane knowledge turned to crafting soul-cages. The vision is intensely personal and horrifying. The character also gains one level of exhaustion and becomes frightened of their own shadow for the next hour, imposing disadvantage on all Wisdom (Perception) checks.
- **On a Success:** The character manages to wrench their gaze away, shaken but unharmed, left with only the lingering dread of the prophecy.   

**Implied Lore:** This mural is the ultimate desecration of the giants' history and philosophy. It shows their Great Silence not as a salvation, but merely a postponement of their doom. It prophesies that their fate is to be consumed, their strength and memory becoming nothing more than fuel for the ascendant [[Lich King]]. It is not a warning they carved; it is a threat delivered by [[Lich King|Eldhoof]] himself—a promise of what is to come.
