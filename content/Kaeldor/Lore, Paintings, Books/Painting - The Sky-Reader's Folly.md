**Appearance:** This painting is done on a massive, circular disc of polished obsidian, thirty feet in diameter, that hangs suspended from the ceiling by immense, frost-covered chains. The "paints" are not pigments, but inlaid veins of precious metals and crushed, glowing minerals. Gold and silver trace the paths of known constellations, while crushed diamonds and sapphires form the glittering stars themselves. The entire piece functions as a giant, static astrolabe.

> **Content:** The painting depicts the night sky from a time long past. The constellations are subtly different, older. In the centre of the disc, a colossal giant is depicted from behind, his form crafted from hammered bronze. He is a "Sky-Reader," a giant astronomer, holding up a complex orrery made of concentric silver rings to the sky.
> The focus of the painting, however, is what he is looking at. In the upper quadrant of the disc, where a familiar constellation should be, there is instead a patch of perfect, unblemished black. It’s not painted black; the obsidian of the disc is simply left bare, polished to a mirror-sheen that reflects nothing. It is a void. The inlaid stars and galaxies of the painting seem to bend and warp around its edges, as if their light is being drawn into it. 

> If a character stares into this void for too long *(requiring a DC 14 Wisdom saving throw)*, they get a fleeting, horrifying mental image of a single, malevolent eye opening in the darkness before the vision is gone.

**Implied Lore:** This artwork is a direct visual link to the _Star-Shaper's Ledger_. It is the giants' attempt to map the cosmic entity they called the "Wanderer" or the "Empty Star." It shows that their knowledge was not limited to the terrestrial; they were aware of cosmic threats. The painting serves as a dire warning and a piece of failed prophecy. They saw the threat coming but were ultimately unable to stop its influence ([[Lich King|Eldhoof]]) from taking root in their world. It tells the party that the enemy they face is merely an agent of something far older and far more terrifying.
