The ledge crumbling. Behind you, a wall of corrupted giants advances, their thunderous steps shaking the very mountain. Before you, a void of churning fog promises a silent end. The blizzard howls, a physical force stealing breath and warmth.

Through the maelstrom, a figure of pure dread emerges. Clad in obsidian armor that pulses with black, sickening energy, he is a wound in the world. Each step he takes scorches the snow to steaming, blackened rock.

[[Oswin]] plants his feet, his great sword a final bastion against the encroaching darkness. He raises it high, and the air crackles as raw magic—a vortex of silver and starlight—is drawn into the blade. He swings.

The sound is of reality tearing. A wave of pure, incandescent energy erupts, vaporizing the snow and carving a deep, cauterized groove into the stone. The mountain, brutalized by the giants and now struck by this incredible force, finally screams its agony. The ground shatters. The cliff edge collapses, and you are plunged into the white abyss.

Weightlessness. The roar of the avalanche is your entire world as you tumble through biting wind and stone. You grasp for a ledge that isn't there, your stomach lurching. This is the end.

But you do not hit bottom.

The fog itself catches you. The mist thickens, becoming tangible, supportive. A soft, ethereal blue nimbus ignites within it, casting your faces in its otherworldly light. The roar of destruction fades to a distant rumble, and the brutal fall becomes a serene descent. You are being cradled by the storm's heart.

Looking up, you see the ragged wound of the cliff face shrinking above you. The dark silhouettes of your foes grow distant, fading like figures in a bad dream.

Above is chaos; here, in this glowing cocoon, is an impossible peace.

The fog peels back like a curtain, not to reveal jagged rocks, but something else entirely. The biting cold is replaced by the scent of aged paper and beeswax. The blue light softens into the warm glow of unseen candles. The sensation of being held gives way to the solid feel of polished wood beneath your feet.

The last wisps of the magical mist curl from your boots and evaporate. You are standing in the profound silence of a vast, circular library, its shelves stretching into the shadowed dome of a ceiling high above. The storm is gone. The mountain is gone. Only the quiet and the dust remain.

The vertigo of your fall vanishes, replaced by the unyielding certainty of polished wood beneath your feet. The roar of the mountain avalanche is gone, swallowed by a profound and unnerving silence. The air, once filled with biting frost, is now still, dry, and heavy with the scent of aged paper, leather, and beeswax.

Before you stretches a vast, circular chamber. Towering shelves, burdened with countless books, curve away into the shadows, rising so high you cannot see their tops. The only light comes from the last vestiges of the ethereal blue fog that saved you, which now pools and dissolves around your ankles, its glow reflecting faintly on the spines of a thousand forgotten stories. You are no longer on a mountain. You are in a library at the heart of nowhere.

## The Frozen Library of Jotunheim

### The Passage of Stillness

The last wisps of ethereal fog evaporate from your boots, leaving you in a silence so profound it feels like a physical pressure. You stand at the mouth of a long, perfectly straight hallway, carved from a material that resembles polished, black obsidian but radiates a deep, soul-sapping cold. The passage is easily thirty feet high and just as wide, clearly built for inhabitants of a grander scale.

There is no discernible light source, yet a faint, blue nimbus emanates from colossal runes etched into the impossibly smooth walls. The light pulses softly, in rhythm with your heartbeats, casting long, distorted shadows that seem to dance just at the edge of your vision. The air is utterly still and frigid, not with the biting wind of the mountain, but with a dead, stagnant cold that promises no warmth will ever return. Your footsteps, which should echo in such a space, are swallowed by the oppressive quiet. Even your own breathing sounds muted and distant, as if the hall itself is holding its breath.

As you proceed, you notice some of the runes are smaller, forming fragmented phrases in the giantish tongue. A successful Arcana check or a simple translation spell reveals their ominous, recurring messages:

- _“Memory is the prison.”_
		 ### 1. When “Memory is the prison.” is spoken:
		**Thematic Meaning:** For the giants, their memories are a self-inflicted torment—an eternal, silent reliving of their greatest failure and sorrow. They are imprisoned by their past. To speak this phrase is to invite the weight of that immense prison upon oneself.
		**Magical Effect:** The moment the final word is spoken, the blue light from the runes on the walls dims, and the air grows heavy. The speaker must immediately make a **DC 15 Charisma saving throw**.
		- **On a Failure:** The character is flooded with a torrent of alien, sorrowful memories that are not their own. For a brief, overwhelming moment, they _are_ a giant watching the sky crack open during the [[#Great Sundering]]. They feel the shame of watching their kin kneel to the Crownless Voice. They experience the crushing despair of their king casting his crown into the sea. The psychic weight is staggering; the character takes **3d8 psychic damage** and is **stunned** until the end of their next turn, their mind trapped in the giants' prison of memory.
		- **On a Success:** The character manages to fight off the worst of the psychic assault but is still shaken by the echoes of immense sorrow. They take half damage and are not stunned.

- _“Silence is salvation.”_
			- ### When “Silence is salvation.” is spoken:		
		**Thematic Meaning:** This is the central pillar of the giants' doctrine. It is their shield, their prayer, and their solution to the world's chaos. To speak this phrase is to align oneself with the library's purpose, invoking its protective power.		
		**Magical Effect:** Upon speaking this phrase, the runes on the walls nearest to the speaker glow with a steady, soft white light. A magical aura emanates from the speaker, creating a **20-foot-radius sphere of profound silence**, identical to the effect of the _Silence_ spell. This sphere is centred on the speaker and moves with them.		
		- Within this sphere, no sound can be created or pass through. Any creature or object entirely inside the sphere is immune to thunder damage, and creatures are deafened while entirely inside it.		    
		- This effect is not hostile. It is a sanctuary. The **Stone Watcher** statues will not enter the sphere, treating it as a wall of pure energy. If the sphere is cast upon them, they will become motionless until it passes.		    
		- The effect lasts for **1 minute**, or until the original speaker uses an action to dismiss it. This effect can only be triggered once per hour.
	
- _“To speak is to break.”_
		- **Thematic Meaning:** This is a direct and literal warning. To the giants, the act of speaking is what broke their world, their people, and their king's heart. Speech is a violent, shattering force that undoes the stillness of existence.
		- **Magical Effect:** The instant this phrase is uttered, it is met with a violent rebuke from the library's wards. The runes on the walls flash with angry, violet-white light, and a wave of pure, silent force erupts from the speaker.
		- Every creature within a **15-foot cube originating from the speaker** (including the speaker themselves) must make a **DC 16 Constitution saving throw**.
		- **On a Failure:** A creature takes **4d10 force damage** and is pushed 15 feet away from the speaker. The force feels like a brutal, physical blow that cracks the very air, threatening to shatter bones.  
		- **On a Success:** A creature takes half as much damage and is not pushed.
		- This is the library's most aggressive defence, treating the speaker as a source of destructive chaos that must be violently silenced.

The hallway ends in a grand, open archway, beyond which the oppressive dark gives way to a vast, open space.

### The Athenaeum of Echoes & The Stone Watchers

Stepping through the archway, the space explodes into a cavernous, circular chamber so immense the far walls are lost in shadow. This is the heart of the library. Towering shelves, carved from the same black ice, spiral upwards into a darkness that conceals the ceiling. They are not burdened with books, but with countless stone tablets, massive obelisks covered in spiralling runes, and scrolls encased in crystalline frost, each item a repository of knowledge measured in centuries. The air is thick with the scent of cold stone and ancient, undisturbed dust—or rather, fine ice crystals that hang suspended in the air, glittering like captured stars in the faint blue glow of the wall-runes.

Lining the aisles and standing in silent contemplation before the shelves are statues. But they are not the colossal figures of giants you might expect. They are perfectly rendered, unnervingly lifelike statues of giants, but carved to be exactly human-sized. Each one is a miniature masterpiece of a lost race: 

a robed scholar with one hand outstretched as if to impart knowledge, 
a hunched scribe with a chisel frozen over a tablet, 
a stern-faced sentinel leaning on a stone spear. 

Their expressions are ones of intense concentration, profound sorrow, or eternal vigilance.

They are utterly still, their stone eyes staring into eternity. Yet, there is a dreadful potential in their stillness. When you look at the scholar and then turn your gaze to the sentinel across the aisle, you feel an unnerving certainty that the scholar’s head was tilted a different way just a moment before. When you all look away to inspect a particularly large tablet, the faint, grinding scrape of stone on ice echoes from behind you. When you turn back, the hunched scribe is now five feet closer, its empty eyes fixed upon you.

These stone watchers are the library's silent curators, and they only move when they are not being observed. They are not immediately hostile, but their slow, inexorable advance fills the chamber with a quiet, creeping menace.

Deeper within this grand chamber, the spiralling shelves begin to grow sparser. The aisles widen, and the walls themselves become the focus. Here, the library transitions from a place of collected knowledge to a gallery of recorded history. The stone watchers are more numerous here, standing in silent clusters, their poses reverent or mournful as they gaze upon the first of the great murals carved into the walls—the visual saga of their people, beginning with the Dawn of [[Old Jotenheim]].


### Atmosphere & Entrance
The party lands in a cavern that isn’t just natural ice — it’s deliberately carved.
Towering shelves of obsidian-like stone rise into darkness, filled with stone tablets, runed obelisks, and frozen scrolls locked inside crystalline cases.
The cold here isn’t biting; it’s absolute stillness, a silence that hums in the bones.
* **Lighting:** Dim, bluish glow radiates from faint runes inscribed on the walls. The glow flickers when the PCs move, as if reacting.
* **Sound:** There is none. No wind, no dripping water. Their footsteps echo unnaturally long.
* **Mood:** This is not a place of learning — it is a tomb of memory.

A few are written in spirals that draw the eye inward, forcing a Wisdom save (DC 14) or the PC stares too long, losing d6 minutes of time.

* “Memory is the prison.”
* “The crowned exile spoke, and we listened.”

### The Murals of Old Jotunheim’s Spine
As the PCs push deeper into the ice-hewn corridors, the shelves of the “library” give way to monumental murals carved directly into the walls. Each panel is the height of a house, chiselled with the deliberate, patient hands of giants who intended their story to outlast the fall of kingdoms. The flickering light of the PCs’ torches (or runes) makes the murals seem to move — not literally like the statues in the library, but as if the figures shift between poses when shadows dance across the carvings.

* **Panel 1 – The Dawn of Jotunheim:** Towering giants, robed in furs and holding great runed staves, stand in a circle around a colossal glacier. They are depicted as shepherds of the north, their hands stretched outward, binding wind and storm. Their gods are represented not as figures, but as shapes of natural force: a spiral for the storm, a flame for the forge, an eye for the moon. Inscription (if deciphered): “We were as mountains, unshaken, and our voices were the wind.”
* **Panel 2 – The Great Silence:** The same giants are shown seated on colossal thrones. But their mouths are sealed shut by runes, and their hands are bound in chains of their own making. Around them, smaller races (dwarves, elves, humans) are shown in chaos — warring, burning, fleeing. The giants deliberately turned away from the wars of [[Kaeldor]], choosing silence as their salvation. Their silence was not weakness — it was a vow. Inscription: “Better stillness than slavery. Better silence than corruption.”
* **Panel 3 – The Crownless Voice:** A human-sized figure stands on a dais before the giants, carved disproportionately small yet drawn with immense shadow stretching across the wall. His head bears no crown, but in his hand is a broken scepter. His shadow is crowned with jagged points. His face has been chiseled out, erased by deliberate hammer strikes. Inscription (half-scratched away): “… and he promised that silence could be broken. That eternity could serve.” Implication: This is [[Lich King|Eldhoof]], the lost prince, remembered by the giants as The Crownless Voice.
* **Panel 4 – The Sundering and the New Walls:** The floating city of [[Aeris Gard]] is shown rising into the sky, with jagged cracks splitting the land below. Giants are seen withdrawing behind colossal glacial walls, while their runes flare like dying stars. Twist: Among them, a few giants are depicted leaving the circle — walking southward, marked with violet flames in their chests. These are the first corrupted kin. Inscription: “The [[#Great Sundering]] sundered us also. We closed our voices, but others opened theirs to the dark.”
* **Panel 5 – The Building of New Jotunheim:** Centuries later, smaller, rougher giants are shown carving new halls into the ice — their staves shorter, their postures hunched. The artistry is rougher here, less majestic, as if the skill of carving itself was fading. Where the old murals are precise, elegant, and vast, the new murals are clumsier, their lines uneven, as though carved in haste. Inscription: “The Old City sleeps. The New City stirs. We are echoes of echoes, but we endure.”
* **Panel 6 – The Black Veins (recent addition):** Jagged black lines have been cut violently through the mural, not carved but burned into the stone. These lines crawl like veins across all the panels, twisting the faces of giants, blackening their eyes. The corruption is not art, but vandalism — necrotic magic seared into the ancient murals. It looks like the work of something trying to overwrite history. Inscription (corrupted): “The Crownless Voice is not gone. He waits. He whispers. He commands.”
