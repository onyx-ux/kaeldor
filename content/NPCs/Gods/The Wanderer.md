### The Wander, has only appeared in what seems like a miriad of a million eyes.

**Nature and Origin:** The Wanderer is not a god, nor is it a devil or demon. It is a being from outside the known cosmology, an entity of pure entropy that predates the current reality. If the gods are the architects of existence, the Wanderer is the silence that came before the first word and the void that will follow the last echo. Its true name, if it has one, is unpronounceable, but the ancient giants called it _Myrkstjarna_, the Empty Star, for that is how its presence is perceived: a hole in the fabric of the cosmos.

Its consciousness is utterly alien. It does not desire conquest, worship, or power in a mortal sense. It desires only to _un-make_. It is driven by a cosmic hunger to dissolve existence—matter, energy, consciousness, and magic—back into the perfect, featureless nothingness from which it came. All life is an intolerable noise to it; all creation, a fleeting, offensive stain upon the purity of the void.

**The First War and the Celestial Prison:** When the primordial deities and titans first began to weave the planes of existence, they encountered the Wanderer as a formless, consuming blight. They could not destroy it, for it was anathema to the very concept of existence; to fight it was like trying to stab a shadow. In their final, desperate act of creation, they did not defeat it but _contained_ it.

They wove its prison into the very laws of reality. The "Empty Star" that the giants observed is not a physical place, but the metaphysical anchor point of this cosmic cage. It is a flaw in the heavens, a point where the firmament is thin, and through which the Wanderer's malevolent anti-existence can be perceived. Its apparent movement across the sky, charted by the giants, is not an orbit, but the slow, inexorable decay of its prison as the universe expands and weakens around it.

**The Whispers in the Dark:** Though imprisoned, the Wanderer's influence can seep through the cracks of its cage like a subtle radiation. This influence manifests as whispers on the edge of hearing, as forbidden knowledge in the margins of scorched texts, and as dreams of power granted to the desperate and ambitious. It offers the secrets of magic that corrupts, decays, and breaks down the natural order—necromancy, soul-manipulation, and the power to create undeath—not as a gift, but as a tool. Every undead creature brought into existence is a small victory for the Wanderer, a pocket of anti-life that weakens the vital energies of the world and, by extension, the integrity of its prison.

**[[Lich King|Eldhoof]], the Chosen Vessel:** The embittered Prince [[Lich King|Eldhoof]], in his quest for power to surpass his brother, was the perfect target. He was brilliant, resentful, and willing to listen to any voice that promised him the strength he felt he was owed. The Wanderer offered him the keys to true immortality and dominion over life and death. [[Lich King|Eldhoof]] believed he had found a secret, ultimate source of arcane power. In truth, he was merely the first to answer the void's call.

[[Lich King|Eldhoof]] is the Wanderer's unwitting champion. The violet energy he and his corrupted minions wield is the signature radiation of the Empty Star, a visible manifestation of its un-making power. As the [[Lich King]]'s influence grows and his armies of the dead swell, he is not building an empire for himself, but acting as a cancer on reality, eroding the Wanderer's prison from within. The [[Lich King]]'s victory would be the Wanderer's escape.

**The Giants' Fear:** The ancient giants, with their deep connection to the elemental forces of the world and their advanced understanding of celestial mechanics, were the first mortals to truly comprehend the nature of the Empty Star. They saw it not as a prophecy of a coming evil king, but as a sign of a coming extinction event. Their desperate choice to enact the Great Silence was a last-ditch effort to quarantine themselves—a belief that by silencing their powerful voices and stilling their world-shaping emotions, they would cease to "feed" the Wanderer the ambient psychic and magical energy it thrives on. They chose to become living statues, hoping the cosmic predator would lose interest in their world and its prison would hold. They were wrong.
