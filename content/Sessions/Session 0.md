# Session 0
## The Veil of the Undead

The fire crackles and spits, a lone beacon of warmth against the oppressive dark of the wilderness. The heat pushes back the chill of the night, but it cannot touch the restless feeling that gnaws at the edges of your peace. As you stare into the dancing flames, lost in thought, a sound cuts through the familiar quiet of the woods—a sound that has no place here. It is the distant, mournful tolling of a heavy bell. Some of you might have heard sailors speak of it in hushed tones, the legendary dusk-bell of [[Stonebridge]], but to hear it now, in the heart of the wilds... it is impossible.

A cold dread, sharp and unnerving, washes over you. A second, equally impossible sound follows the first. A low, whistling wind rises, yet the branches of the trees around your camp remain deathly still. This wind seems to bypass the world entirely, whispering directly into your minds. Your eyes are drawn to the edge of the firelight, where an opaque, white gloom begins to gather. It spills between the trees like a silent, ghostly river—the Fog, the unnatural mist from the legends of the "[[The Hour of Silence]]." It is no longer a story told to frighten children. It is here.

The fog creeps closer, swallowing the ancient trees and muffling the sounds of the night until only the crackle of your fire and the phantom wind remain. It carries with it the smell of grave-dirt and forgotten things. From the depths of the mist, you hear a scream, sharp and terrified, cut brutally short. Suddenly, a violent, spectral gust of wind blasts through your campsite. It tears at your cloaks and sends embers scattering, threatening to extinguish your precious fire. The flames dip and sputter, plunging you into near darkness. And in that fleeting dimness, you see them.

Where the firelight now struggles to reach, at the very edge of the encroaching fog, two points of ghostly blue light burn in the gloom. They are eyes, filled with an ancient sorrow, yet fixed upon you with a chilling focus. Then two more appear beside them. And then a dozen more, a constellation of spectral eyes materializing in the darkness, all watching you from the suffocating wall of white.

There is no thought of flight. There is nowhere to run. You are frozen in place as the supernatural gust dies, leaving the world in a ringing silence. Your campfire, your only shield against the night, gutters and sputters violently. Its light recedes, shrinking from a brave circle into a pathetic, flickering pool, abandoning the treeline to the hungry fog. The mist rolls in, not as a vapour, but as a solid, suffocating wall. It devours the space between you, swallowing the sight of your companions until you can no longer see the face of the person sitting an arm's length away. You might open your mouth to call a name, but the sound is instantly deadened, smothered by the oppressive quiet. With a final, mournful hiss, the last ember of your fire is extinguished by the ghostly cold.

You are plunged into absolute, impenetrable blackness. The eerie silence of the forest is now total. There is only the damp chill of the fog clinging to you like a burial shroud, the frantic thunder of your own heart in your ears, and the horrifying certainty that you are blind, utterly alone, and yet, surrounded. The eyes are no longer in the distance; you feel their gaze upon you from all sides in the suffocating dark.

That is when the words find you. They do not echo off the walls but form directly in your mind, spoken in a voice that breaks your heart. It is the voice of someone you loved, someone you know was lost in the [[#Great Sundering]]—a parent, a lover, a sibling, a friend. The voice is icy cold, yet it carries all the warmth of their memory as it whispers a single, desperate plea.

“Save us.”

For [[Blake]], alone even in this shared moment, the voice is not one he has ever heard in life, but one he knows from sorrowful portraits and the heavy silence in his father's halls. It is the voice of his mother, [[Annelise]], a gentle whisper from a life lost not to the [[#Great Sundering]] itself, but to the succession crisis it created.

For [[Visage]], there is no voice from this world. Instead, a profound and alien silence presses in, a psychic weight that is the echo of a song that has ended. It is the feeling of a single, stray note left hanging in a vast and empty cathedral. In this crushing stillness, he feels a phantom pang of loss for a choir he can't remember, a sorrow that isn't his own but is ancient and immense.

For [[Tyris]], the voice is a phantom of the home he abandoned, the one he now knows was consumed by the war. It is the voice of his mother, [[Namine]], who he believed was safe in the heart of [[Valdrak]].

For [[Aolis]], it is not a single voice that pierces the silence. It is a sudden, sickening vibration from the dead branch tucked away in his pack, which pulses with a cold, desperate energy. The faint, sighing whispers he sometimes hears from the wood amplify into a chorus of agony the sound of a million dying leaves, of ancient bark cracking, of a god-tree weeping. It is the voice of the [[World Tree]] itself, a psychic scream of pain that coalesces into a single, desperate plea.

For [[Magni]], the voice that cuts through the silence is the one that sometimes whispers from the haunted steel of his greataxe. It is the voice of his younger brother, [[Duramir Darkforge]], who fell beside the rest of his kin when [[Valdrak]] was devoured.

A tempest of sorrow, a symphony of despair, crashes upon the shores of your consciousness. Before you, a scene of heart-wrenching anguish unfolds. [[King Illhoof]], his noble face a mask of grief so profound it seems to carve new lines into his very soul, stands upon a precipice overlooking the grand city of [[Valdrak]]. Each spire, each testament to dwarven and goblin ingenuity, is a dagger twisting in his heart. His voice, when it comes, is not a roar of command, but a choked whisper surrendering to the inevitable. "Abandon the city," he breathes, and the words are a death sentence.

Around you, the world begins to unravel. Mages, their faces grim and streaked with tears, raise their hands to the heavens, their voices a chorus of arcane power and desperate pleas. The very bedrock of the world groans in protest, a deep, resonant tremor that shakes the foundations of [[Valdrak]] and your own sanity. With a sound that is both a crack of thunder and the shattering of a thousand hearts, the city begins to shrink, a jewel of civilization receding into the distance as if being swallowed by the very earth it was built upon.

In the blink of an eye, you are violently returned to the ground, the dust and chaos of a city in its death throes filling your senses. Panic, a feral beast, is unleashed upon the populace. The stout-hearted dwarves and the cunning goblins of [[Valdrak]], their faces etched with a terror that transcends reason, flock to the [[Great Forge of Moradin]], a desperate, surging tide of bodies seeking a final sanctuary.

But there is no sanctuary. From the shadows, they come. The Undead. A relentless, shambling horde that pours through the boulevards of [[Valdrak]], their progress marked by the chilling silence of the grave and the screams of the living. And in their ranks, you see them. The cold, icy eyes from your fragmented visions, a malevolent blue glow that promises only oblivion. Yet now, that chilling cobalt flickers, shifting in a horrifying dance with a piercing, rage-filled red. It is the color of bloodlust, of a hatred so pure and undiluted it burns through the icy grip of death itself.

You turn, a desperate instinct to flee, but your escape is cut short by a wall of obsidian plate mail. A blade, cold as the grave and twice as sharp, erupts from your chest in a spray of crimson. Your gaze is drawn upwards, to the face of your killer. A death knight, a monument to damnation, his armor a testament to the dark forges of the [[Lich King]]'s army. Upon his helm, a three-pointed crown sits atop a leering skull, the unmistakable crest of the Scourge.

The world dissolves into a searing agony, your breath a ragged, wet gasp. And then… nothing.
You find yourself standing, impossibly, in the heart of [[Valdrak]] once more. The air is thick with the scent of hot metal and desperation. You are in the [[Great Forge of Moradin]]. Around you, dwarves, their faces a mixture of terror and fervent faith, are on their knees, their prayers to the Soulforger a desperate chant against the encroaching darkness. Others, consumed by a primal panic, scramble towards the inner caverns, their cries echoing in the vast, vaulted chamber.

A sense of disorientation washes over you as you look around, your phantom wound a chilling memory. And in the flickering light of the forge, you see them. The other members of your party, their faces reflecting the same confusion, the same dawning horror. You are together in this nightmare, bound by a fate as terrible and as mysterious as the cataclysm that has just torn your world asunder.
