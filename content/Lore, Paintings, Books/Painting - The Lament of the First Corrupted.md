**Appearance:** This is not a canvas painting, but a colossal fresco plastered onto a concave wall, spanning a hundred feet in width. The colours are muted earth tones—ochre, charcoal, and terracotta—but they are shot through with veins of a sickening, vibrant violet that seems to pulse faintly in the low light. The entire fresco is covered in a thin, clear layer of magical ice, preserving it perfectly but making the figures seem distant, as if viewed through a frozen tear.

>**Content:** The scene is one of tragic supplication. A giant, easily fifty feet tall even while kneeling, is the central figure. His body is a masterpiece of anatomical detail, all coiled muscle and weary strength, but his face is a mask of anguish. From a wound in his chest—a perfectly circular hole where his heart should be—pours not blood, but a torrent of the foul violet energy that stains the rest of the mural. His massive hands are clasped together, not in prayer, but in a desperate plea.
>Before him stands the disproportionately small, human-sized figure of the **Crownless Voice**, [[Lich King|Eldhoof]]. He is depicted not as a lich, but as the handsome, charismatic prince he once was. His form is rendered entirely in shadow, a void in the fresco, but one hand is extended, touching the giant's forehead. Where his fingers make contact, the giant's flesh has turned to blackened, cracked stone, and one of the giant's eyes weeps the same violet light. Around them, the pristine, geometric beauty of what must have been Old [[Old Jotenheim]] is shown with jagged cracks spiderwebbing across its foundations, mirroring the corruption of its first defender.

**Implied Lore:** This painting captures the moment the giants' vow of silence was broken not by their own will, but by the first of their kind falling to [[Lich King|Eldhoof]]'s influence. It’s a piece of propaganda and a memorial, showing that the giants see the corrupted not as traitors, but as victims of a profound spiritual sickness. It is a visual representation of the line from the murals: _"We closed our voices, but others opened theirs to the dark."_
