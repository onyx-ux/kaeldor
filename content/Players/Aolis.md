 # 🧝 Aolis
## Name: Aolis
* **Race:** Eladrin
* **Class:** Circle of the Land Druid

## Backstory: Aolis the Elf

[[Aolis]] was a strange young elf, born among the upper noble ranks to [[Arathorn]] and [[Myrin]] of the old [[House Saucier]], merchants of old who traveled far and wide to bring trade across the continent of [[Kaeldor/WorldInfo/Kaeldor]]. [[Aolis]]'s journey began in the ancient trees of the elven enclave of [[Myth Aelune]], where nature was worshiped and tradition ruled. While his people communed with spirits and guarded sacred groves, [[Aolis]] found his heart stirred by something very different than the tedious lessons of trade and merchanting. It was not ink or coin that called to him, it was the heat of the kitchens and the aromas of spice.

He would sneak into the community kitchens, stealing herbs meant for rituals and spices meant for tea, blending them with berries, mushrooms, and roots to create wild, daring concoctions that made elders scoff and younglings whisper about the mad young noble. His father never approved of these outings or experiments. [[Aolis]], with hair like starlight and fingers meant for harp strings and scrolls, was expected to follow the path of the merchant, like his forefathers before him. But [[Aolis]] had a different craving, not for power, but for flavor. He would often sneak away from the grand libraries to gather wild herbs, roast roots over an open flame, and attempt to mimic the flavors he remembered from wandering markets.

When a diplomatic envoy brought [[Aolis]] to the region of the [[Ash-Veil Peaks]] for a rare merchant summit between [[Stonebridge]] and [[Aeris Gard]], his curiosity led him not to the plush diplomats’ couches and meeting rooms, but to the heart of the old castle: the dwarven kitchen halls. There, he met [[Bruenor Stonehand]], a battle-scarred dwarf with a beard like braided fire and a reputation not only as a fearsome warrior but as the best cook this side of the Peaks. [[Bruenor Stonehand]] had once fed an entire battalion on nothing but mushrooms, ale, and sheer stubbornness during the [[Siege of Khaz’Modan]].

At first, the dwarf had no patience for the “twig, limbed flower eater” poking around his hearth. [[Aolis]], with his soft hands and poetic descriptions of flavor, grated on [[Bruenor Stonehand]]’s nerves. But when the elf produced a delicate wine reduction sauce to pair with spit, roasted boar, and it actually worked, the dwarf’s curiosity was piqued. Over weeks, what began as reluctant tolerance turned into grudging respect. [[Bruenor]] taught [[Aolis]] how to season with instinct, not incantations, and how to handle a cleaver with the same confidence as a sword. [[Aolis]], in turn, introduced the dwarf to subtlety: how a hint of mint could brighten a stew, or how to layer textures like composing a ballad. They argued constantly, so much so one would have mistaken them for a couple of bickering kids rather than a teacher and his wayward apprentice. Over the right cut of meat. Over whether garlic should be crushed or minced. Over whether ale or wine made a better stew base. But through it all, the fire never died down, and neither did their friendship.

When the summit ended, [[Aolis]] returned to [[Myth Aelune]] with more than just a trade agreement. He brought back recipes, burns on his hands, and a fondness for dwarven spice blends no elf could comprehend. He also carried a small rune, etched knife and a bag that held more than what one’s eyes could discern, two gifts from [[Bruenor]], engraved with the words: “For the elf who learned to cook with steel., B.S.”

Seasons passed. [[Aolis]], now more grounded and practical than most elves dared to be, exchanged letters with [[Bruenor]] regularly. Their correspondence, scrolls and scraps smeared with grease and ink, was filled with recipes, playful insults, and the occasional sketch of a new dish. [[Bruenor]]’s letters were unmistakable. They came on thick parchment that smelled faintly of smoke and iron, sealed with a crude wax imprint of a frying pan. Always brief. Always gruff. And always deeply comforting in their own strange way.

But then, they stopped. A month passed. Then two. [[Aolis]] wrote again, first with humor, then with concern, and eventually with a quiet desperation. He sent messages through dwarven merchants, inquired with travelers, even tried scrying, though [[Bruenor]] always mocked “fancy elf tricks,” and had likely placed some ward to block magical peeking. But after all that the results remained the same, he had nothing. The [[Ironspine]] halls had gone dark to him.

It wasn’t until the third moon of the frost season that a battered, soot, stained dwarf arrived at the edge of [[Myth Aelune]]. His name was [[Grunrik Embergut]], a former stonemason and friend of [[Bruenor]]’s. He brought no letter, only [[Bruenor]]’s old meat cleaver, wrapped in tattered linen, and a grim tale. There had been tremors deep in the [[Ironspine]]. A new mining crew had unearthed something beneath the oldest tunnels, something ancient, silent, and wrong. Fires went out for no reason. Shadows lingered too long. One by one, miners vanished. Then [[Bruenor]], always the first to answer a challenge and the last to back down, went into the depths with a small crew. None of them returned.

“We held vigil,” [[Grunrik]] said, his eyes dull. “But the mountain… it’s not right anymore. Even the forges have gone cold.”

The cleaver was heavy in [[Aolis]]’s hands. It still bore nicks from decades of use, the hilt worn smooth by [[Bruenor]]’s calloused grip. [[Aolis]] didn’t weep, not then, not now. He thanked [[Grunrik]] for what he had shown him and guided him to a room to rest after having come such a long way with such ill news. Instead, he did what [[Bruenor]] would have wanted, he cooked a simple meal that his teacher had always said was his favorite. That night, beneath the great silver trees, he lit a fire. Not the gentle flame of elven lanterns, but a roaring blaze built from pine and stubbornness. He roasted mountain hare over it, seasoned with salt and ironbark pepper, the way [[Bruenor]] had taught him. He placed the cleaver beside the fire and whispered a dwarven prayer he had memorized but never fully understood. Then he ate alone and in silence.

The next morning, [[Aolis]] packed his satchel. He took only what he needed: dried supplies, the rune, etched knife and [[Bruenor]]’s cleaver. He told no one where he was going, not the trees or his own family, he left a note with a simple goodbye and a simple request for forgiveness. The [[Ironspine]] had gone quiet, but [[Aolis]] knew that silence wasn’t always final. Not for dwarves. And certainly not for [[Bruenor Stonehand]], the most stubborn soul he’d ever known. He hoped his mother and father would understand what drove him to do this, and that they would forgive him upon his return. But one thing was set in his heart: He was going to find [[Bruenor Stonehand]] and open the restaurant they both always dreamed of. Or at the very least, bring him home and lay him to rest.

## 👥 People of Note
* **[[Arathorn]]** and **[[Myrin]]**: [[Aolis]]'s parents, noble merchants of [[House Saucier]].
* **[[Bruenor Stonehand]]**: A battle-scarred dwarf and renowned cook who teaches [[Aolis]]. He goes missing after investigating strange occurrences in the [[Ironspine]].
* **[[Grunrik Embergut]]**: A former stonemason and friend of [[Bruenor]]'s who delivers the news of [[Bruenor]]'s disappearance to [[Aolis]].
* **[[Bramthorn Emberpan]]**: head chef to the royal family who I send letters for advice, in a friendly relationship and have met a couple of times while sneaking into the kitchens at the big noble meetings.
* **[[Vaelrix Saucier]]**: twin brother to my father who while always being the fun uncle who tells me to follow my dreams has always secretly resented my father for inheriting the position of lordship over him.
* **[[Malrion Saucier]]**: Patriarch to the [[House Saucier]] and the personification of stubborn, has always maintained the house no matter what the cost and it willing to help keep the status of house saucier even if it means doing less the legal things to keep our position as the major trade faction within our walls.

## The Vision of the Flawed Heartwood

**The Trigger:** The vision comes to [[Aolis]] during a moment of quiet reflection, perhaps as she is tending to her gear by the firelight, or in the profound silence of the giant's library. She draws her ceremonial knife—the one given to her in the name of [[Moradin]] to clean its blade. As she polishes the steel, the runes etched upon it flare with an intense, internal heat, and the world around her dissolves into the roar of a celestial forge.

**The Vision's Setting:** [[Aolis]] is no longer in the library, but stands upon an anvil the size of a feasting hall. Around her stretches a smithy built into the heart of a mountain, so vast that the ceiling is a constellation of cooling stars. The air hums with a power that makes her teeth ache, and the heat from a colossal forge-fire warms her soul, chasing away the gnawing cold of [[Old Jotenheim]]. The rhythmic, earth-shaking **CLANG** of a hammer striking steel is the only sound, each impact sending a tremor through the very firmament of the vision.

Before the forge stands [[Moradin]]. He is not the dwarven king of legend, but the god in his full glory. His form is immense, carved from living granite, with a beard of woven magma that drips sparks onto the anvil floor. He works tirelessly, his hammer striking a length of what appears to be pure, white-hot starlight, shaping it with divine purpose. He does not turn to face her, but his voice echoes in her mind, as powerful and resonant as the hammer's blow.

**The Divine Message:** _"Daughter of the Stone,"_ the voice of [[Moradin]] booms, not with anger, but with the focused intensity of a master craftsman. _"You have seen the prophecies. The twin branches of what is to come. You have seen the Verdant Canticle and the Ashen Codex. Both are true steel. The final shape depends on the smith's hand."_

He lifts the glowing bar of starlight from the anvil with tongs of silver and plunges it into the forge-fire, which erupts in a shower of golden sparks.

_"Deep in the heart of this world, a seed sleeps. A Heartwood. The First Forge from which all life was given its shape. It is the work of the old gods, a masterpiece of creation intended to be the world's final bastion against the cold, empty dark."_

His expression, reflected in the polished surface of his hammer, grows grim. The fire in the forge seems to dim for a moment.

_"But the work was flawed. When the world was given its first shape, a splinter from the Great Un-making—the void that came before—was trapped within the steel. A flaw. A bubble of absolute nothingness, a seed of purest corruption, buried deep within the Heartwood. It is a cold that quenches all forges, a silence that smothers all songs."_

He pulls the starlight from the fire, now glowing with an almost unbearable intensity. He lays it on the anvil and gestures to it with his hammer. [[Aolis]] sees it now—at the very center of the incandescent bar is a single, tiny, spiderweb crack of absolute blackness. A flaw that, if struck, would shatter the entire work.

_"The Ashen King, the [[Lich King]], does not know the location of the Heartwood, but he knows of the flaw. He seeks it. He desires to strike that single point of weakness, to pour his master's poison into the crack and turn the First Forge into an engine of undeath. He would make the Ashen Codex a reality."_

[[Moradin]]'s gaze finally meets hers, his eyes like molten gold. The weight of his attention is immense, a pressure of divine will.

_"The blade you hold was forged in my name. It is a key, a compass, a weapon. But its true purpose is to be a smith's tool. You, [[Aolis]], are my shield at the forge. You must find the Heartwood before he does. Guard it. Protect it. And when the time comes, and the flaw is revealed... you must be the one to strike it clean from the steel. Do not let the masterpiece be broken."_

With a final, deafening **CLANG**, he brings his hammer down upon the bar of starlight. The impact is not on the flaw, but beside it. The force is so great that the vision shatters into a billion points of light.
